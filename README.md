## 🌟 Overview

ApexPit is not another bloated toolbox pretending to be a racing assistant. It is a focused, carefully engineered companion layer that sits beside your F1 23 installation and offers granular control over the aspects of the simulation most players never get to touch. Whether you want to experiment with grip behaviour, dial in tyre degradation curves, or simply enjoy the sandbox nature of your own racing journey, ApexPit gives you the levers — and steps out of the way.

The project began as a weekend experiment shared among a small Discord of sim-racing friends. It grew, quietly, into a full-fledged open-source companion used across dozens of countries. In 2026, ApexPit remains volunteer-maintained, transparent in its code, and committed to the principle that your game is *yours* to shape.

## 💡 Why ApexPit Exists

Modern racing sims are beautifully tuned — and rigidly locked. ApexPit was born from a simple observation: some of the most satisfying moments in F1 23 come from bending the rules of the simulation in your own favour, just to see what happens. ApexPit packages that curiosity into something safe, reversible, and easy to walk away from.

Rather than chasing an endgame, ApexPit embraces the *sandbox* philosophy: change a dial, watch the telemetry shift, revert instantly. It's a laboratory for your racing instincts.

## 🎨 Interface & Experience

The ApexPit UI was designed by someone who spends more time in a cockpit than a spreadsheet. Menus are grouped by *intent* rather than by technical category: "Make it grippier", "Make tyres last", "Make it chaotic". Beneath each intent lives the full complement of sliders for advanced users, but the top layer is deliberately human.

Highlights of the interface:

- Ambience-aware dark theme that respects Windows accent colours.
- Keyboard-first navigation for players who dislike alt-tabbing.
- Toast-style notifications instead of modal popups — nothing interrupts your session.
- A "Race Engineer Notes" panel that explains, in plain language, what each setting does.

## ⚙️ Performance Tuning Options

ApexPit exposes controls across categories that map to the deeper simulation layers. Each is documented in-game with tooltips and a short story about why it exists.

- **Grip & Handling** — Surface friction multipliers, steering response curves, suspension softness.
- **Tyre Model** — Degradation slope, thermal response, wear visual fidelity.
- **Weather & Track** — Precipitation intensity, track temperature drift, rubber layering rate.
- **AI Behaviour** — Aggression, consistency, radio chatter cadence.
- **Camera & Presentation** — FOV defaults, motion blur scale, HUD opacity.
- **Session Rules** — Practice length, qualifying format, formation lap toggle.

Every setting is reversible and stored in a plain JSON journal file you can inspect, edit, or delete.

## 🔧 Behind the Scenes

ApexPit runs as a signed companion process alongside your game. It never modifies game binaries on disk. Instead, it uses a session-scoped injection layer that evaporates the moment you close the game — leaving your installation byte-identical to how you found it.

Key engineering principles:

1. **Transparency first.** The full source is in this repository. Read it, audit it, argue with it.
2. **Local-only operation.** No telemetry leaves your machine. No accounts. No cloud sync.
3. **Fail-safe by default.** If ApexPit encounters an unexpected state, it reverts and stays silent until you restart.
4. **Deterministic builds.** Release artefacts are reproducible from tagged commits.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Will this affect online races?**
A: No. ApexPit operates only in offline sessions. It detects online lobbies and disables itself automatically.

**Q: Do I need to keep it open while racing?**
A: You launch ApexPit first, then the game. The overlay can be hidden once you're satisfied with your tuning.

**Q: Is my progress safe?**
A: Yes. ApexPit never writes to your save files. Profiles are stored separately.

**Q: Something broke — what now?**
A: Open an issue with the log file attached. Moderators respond around the clock.

**Q: Can I share my presets?**
A: Absolutely. Preset files are portable JSON and encouraged as community contributions.
